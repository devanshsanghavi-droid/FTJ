# Study 3 results: sandboxed Shopify pixels vs consent choices

Analysis of the live crawl of 2026-09-26 (residential vantage point, California, USA), revised the same day after five internal reviews (`review/figure_fixes.md`). Every number in this file is rendered by `analysis/write_results.py` from `analysis/outputs/stats.json` and is checked by `analysis/trace_check.py` (result in `analysis/outputs/trace_check.txt`). Rates are store-level shares of the main sample unless stated. Brackets are 95% confidence intervals. Wilson intervals are primary: they describe the sampled stores and treat them as a draw from a super-population of such stores, without a finite-population correction; because the three upper buckets are censuses, corrected intervals for the frame would be narrower (projection of banner stores firing before any choice: 14.0–17.3% with the correction, 13.5–17.8% without), so the reported intervals are conservative. Subgroup, per-product and per-vendor intervals are descriptive and not adjusted for multiplicity. For headline rates a cluster bootstrap by consent tool (BCa, 2,000 resamples; 593 clusters, 572 of them single stores without a consent tool) is reported as a sensitivity interval, because clustering by tool is not part of the sampling design and the three largest tools hold 62% of banner stores. Shares whose unit is a request, a pixel entry, a pixel start or a store-vendor pair are clustered within stores, so they carry intervals that resample stores (a percentile bootstrap, or the Korn-Graubard interval where marked); kappa also resamples stores. Paired risk differences use Newcombe's hybrid-score interval for paired data, and independent ones his interval for two samples.

## 1. Headline findings

1. **Before any choice.** A sandboxed ad pixel sent at least one request (a data beacon or a library load) before any choice on 803 of 1,362 (59.0%, 95% CI 56.3–61.5%; cluster bootstrap by consent tool 55.6–62.1%) stores. A consent banner appeared on 410 of 1,362 (30.1%, 95% CI 27.7–32.6%; cluster bootstrap by consent tool 18.6–47.1%). Among banner stores the rate was 205 of 410 (50.0%, 95% CI 45.2–54.8%; cluster bootstrap by consent tool 32.7–58.8%), against 598 of 952 (62.8%, 95% CI 59.7–65.8%) of stores without a banner. At our California vantage point Shopify's region default was 'allowed until choice' on 1,106 of 1,353 (81.7%, 95% CI 79.6–83.7%) of stores that returned one; among banner stores the pre-choice rate was 165 of 236 (69.9%, 95% CI 63.8–75.4%) where the default allowed and 40 of 167 (24.0%, 95% CI 18.1–31.0%) where it denied.
2. **Before the banner is even visible.** The banner detector polls every 250 ms. On 108 of 205 (52.7%, 95% CI 45.9–59.4%) of the 205 banner stores with a pre-choice sandboxed request, the first such request came more than one polling interval before the banner was detected (primary, conservative); on 151 of 205 (73.7%, 95% CI 67.2–79.2%) it came before at all. Counting only data beacons, 135 of 200 (67.5%, 95% CI 60.7–73.6%). The per-store flag repeats only moderately: 36 of 46 repeat stores agreed (kappa 0.54), with median absolute timing shifts of 414 ms (first request) and 291 ms (banner) between the two loads.
3. **Blocked on paper.** 21 stores (3 consent tools: 15 Pandectes, 5 OneTrust, 1 Cookiebot) have a readable blocklist that names an ad vendor as blocked before any choice. A blocklist is in force for a visitor only while the CMP has not consented for that visitor, and on 7 of these stores the CMP itself wrote 'marketing: yes' to Shopify in the Default load without any click (2 of them through Pandectes' rule for automated browsers, see 'What the pages saw of our crawler' below). **Primary (revised after referee review):** on the 14 stores whose CMP wrote no 'yes', the blocklisted vendor's own pixel fired from Shopify's sandbox before any choice on 7 of 14 (50.0%, 95% CI 26.8–73.2%); any sandboxed ad pixel fired on 8/14. Unconditioned, over all 21 stores: 12 of 21 (57.1%, 95% CI 36.5–75.5%) (by any route 16/21; Pandectes 9/15, OneTrust 3/5, Cookiebot 0/1). Conditioning instead on installation, on the 15 stores with a configured sandboxed pixel of a declared vendor it fired on 10 of 15 (66.7%, 95% CI 41.7–84.8%) (6/11 of those whose CMP wrote no 'yes'); on 4 of the 21 stores no declared vendor had a sandboxed pixel at all. **The blocklist made no measurable difference to sandboxed firing:** comparing any sandboxed ad pixel like for like within Shopify's region default, among stores whose CMP wrote no 'yes', blocklist stores fired on 6/7 vs 98/139 banner stores without a readable declaration where the default allowed marketing, and on 2/7 vs 24/145 where it denied (Mantel-Haenszel difference 13.6 pp, 95% CI -8.4 to 35.6); over all stores 9/10 vs 123/174 and 6/11 vs 36/158 (difference 25.8 pp, 8.0 to 43.6). This tests the falsification criterion of pre-crawl prediction P1: the data are consistent with no reduction and rule out reductions larger than 8 percentage points. What decided firing was Shopify's state: the declared vendor fired on 8/10 blocklist stores whose default allowed marketing and 4/11 whose default denied it, and 3 of those 4 were stores whose CMP had switched the denying default to 'yes'. **Direct evidence:** in one later re-check load per store, the blocker neutralized a declared vendor's main-document tag while that vendor's pixel sent requests from Shopify's sandbox in the same load on 5 stores (S0165, S0213, S0219, S0370, S0697; none a bot-rule store); and (exploratory) on 8 stores the CMP's own blocklist pattern matches a URL that the sandbox fetched in the Default load (13 of 18 Pandectes entries whose vendor fired from the sandbox; 3 of these stores wrote no 'yes'). A blocker neutralized at least one element in the re-check on 18 of 21 (85.7%, 95% CI 65.4–95.0%) blocklist stores; of the 12 stores where the declared vendor had fired, 2 OneTrust stores (S0589, S0698) showed no active blocker, so OneTrust's declaration may not be enforced there. Where the banner itself told Shopify 'marketing: no' before any choice, a sandboxed ad pixel had already fired on 39 of 66 (59.1%, 95% CI 47.0–70.1%).
4. **The race.** In 147 loads where the CMP wrote 'marketing: no' to Shopify without a click, 535 of 545 third-party pixel sandboxes that declare 'marketing' had already started (median 648 ms before the write event); 291 of them started before Shopify's consent script (`consent-tracking-api.js`) was even requested, a lower bound for any write through it. Conversely, in 35 loads the CMP wrote 'marketing: yes' with no click at all, and all 342 gated third-party sandboxes started after it (median 14 ms later). These automatic 'yes' writes are not all behaviour a visitor would meet: some are the CMP's reaction to an automated browser (see 'What the pages saw of our crawler').
5. **Decline.** 165 of 407 (40.5%, 95% CI 35.9–45.4%) banners had no reject option on the first layer. Of 242 clicks, 9 forced clicks failed (banner still shown and no refusal recorded; blocked by a modal, an age gate or a chat widget) and are excluded. After the 233 clicks that worked, Shopify recorded an explicit refusal on 198 of 233 (85.0%, 95% CI 79.8–89.0%); on stores whose default allowed marketing, the click turned it off on 94 of 118 (79.7%, 95% CI 71.5–85.9%). On a fresh page (the reload after the click), a newly created sandbox still sent an ad request on 53 of 233 (22.7%, 95% CI 17.8–28.5%; cluster bootstrap by consent tool 17.8–29.7%) of stores, against 45.5% under Default in the same window on the same stores (paired reduction 22.7 pp (95% CI 17.2 to 28.1); every fresh-page residual store also fired under Default, so about half of the Default-positive stores kept firing: 53/106); counting any time after the click it was 80 of 233 (34.3%, 95% CI 28.5–40.6%). The fresh-page residual was 9 of 57 (15.8%, 95% CI 8.5–27.4%) with Shopify's own banner, 32 of 144 (22.2%, 95% CI 16.2–29.7%) with third-party tools that write to Shopify, and 12 of 32 (37.5%, 95% CI 22.9–54.7%) with blocker-only or unknown tools.
6. **GPC.** Shopify recorded 'sale of data: no' on 1,169 of 1,172 (99.7%, 95% CI 99.3–99.9%) GPC loads that returned a state. Of 7,478 configured pixel entries that declare sale of data, 0 started under GPC, in 1,268 at-risk loads (upper 95% bound 0.3% of loads; the entry-level bound 0.05% treats entries as independent and is not used). Still, of the stores with sandboxed ad requests under Default, 279 of 803 (34.7%, 95% CI 31.5–38.1%; cluster bootstrap by consent tool 30.9–38.7%) kept sending them under GPC, all from pixels that do not declare sale of data.
7. **Decline vs GPC.** Estimand: persistence after the choice, on a later page (a sandbox created after the choice; equal 10 s windows). On the same 233 stores: Decline 53 vs GPC 41 stores, a paired difference of 5.2 pp (95% CI 0.2 to 10.2) (discordant 23 vs 11, exact McNemar p = 0.058); GPC's advantage in post-choice persistence was thus between about 0 and 10 pp. Against Default in the same window (106 stores), Decline reduced the share by 22.7 pp (95% CI 17.2 to 28.1) and GPC by 27.9 pp (95% CI 22.0 to 33.6). Over the whole visit the controls differ much more: a Decline user is exposed before the click, and a sandboxed ad request came before the click on 101 of these stores, against 41 for the whole GPC load (discordant 61 vs 1; difference 25.8 pp (95% CI 19.9 to 31.5); the pre-click window is the shorter, so this favours Decline). Sensitivity analysis with a different estimand (Decline attempted, including the failed forced clicks): 57 vs 42 of 242, difference 6.2 pp (95% CI 1.3 to 11.2) (p = 0.020). The earlier 'any time after the click' comparison (45 vs 6 discordant) mixed unequal windows and sandboxes that no choice could stop.
8. **Shopify's gate held in almost all observed cases.** Measured on configured pixels at risk: 0 of 7,478 sale-declaring pixel entries started under GPC (0 of 1,268 at-risk loads); under a denying region default 2,220 of 2,248 (98.8%, 95% CI 94.5–99.9%, clustered by store) marketing-declaring pixel entries were held back (loads without a CMP 'yes'; a pixel started in 2/219 loads); as a check of the detector, 8,914 of 8,999 (99.1%, 95% CI 98.1–99.6%, clustered by store) such entries started when marketing was allowed. Among pixel starts made under a partly denied state by pixels that declare a purpose, 97 of 6,992 starts had a declared purpose denied; they came from 10 loads on 7 stores (40, 27, 12 of them from three single stores), so the rate to quote is per store (7 of 1,181 (0.6%, 95% CI 0.3–1.2%) stores with such a start) or per load (10 of 1,560 (0.6%, 95% CI 0.3–1.2%)); the start-level ratio, clustered by store, is 1.4% (95% CI 0.4–3.6%), and its denominator includes starts whose own declared purposes were all allowed, so it is not a violation rate. The 10 loads: 3 edge-cached pages carrying another region's consent string; 5 loads in which the page's own consent string already declined the purpose (S0319__A1, S0552__A1, S1176__A0r, S1176__A2r, S1310__A2); and 2 GPC loads (S0301__A2, S1227__A2) whose first third-party marketing sandbox started 836 and 322 ms after a CMP's automatic 'no' was dispatched, far beyond the clock differences. Neither group is explained by our data; for the second, the manager may have decided before the write, which we did not verify. The first pages of Decline loads are excluded (their consent events were not recorded); they hold 140 further flagged starts in 9 loads.
9. **Google registers refusals in its own layer.** On the next page after a Decline, 357 Google beacons on 116 stores carried Consent Mode 'storage denied' (gcs=G100), 128 on 18 stores 'granted' (G111), and 689 on 94 stores no consent state. Under GPC, Google requests carried the restricted-data-processing flag (rdp) 5,809 times (Default: 20) and the non-personalised-ads flag (npa) 2,914 times (Default: 805). The sandboxed residuals are not explained by such flags: every remaining sandboxed request carried a refusal signal on 9 of 53 stores after Decline and 18 of 279 under GPC.
10. **URLs reach vendors.** 63.4% (95% CI 60.9–66.0%) of 10,676 sandboxed ad beacons (769 stores) in the Default load carried the full landing URL with our test code (all origins: 71.3%). On the creator's Store C page the per-video code reached TikTok from its sandbox in the Default, repeat and pre-click Decline loads; after the Decline and its reload it reached 4 vendors, 0 from a sandbox and none of them TikTok.
11. **Stable and consistent with native GPC.** Repeat Default loads agreed with kappa 0.985 (95% CI 0.961–1.000); emulated GPC and Brave's native GPC agreed on 99/99 stores, of which only 21 were positive; the check discriminates because Default and GPC differed on 39 of these stores and 109 store-vendor pairs, and Brave matched GPC on all (39 and 109). Per-store counts of sandboxed ad requests also repeated: Spearman rho 0.982 (0.956–0.999) for Default vs repeat and 0.999 (0.996–1.000) for GPC vs repeat (274 stores).
12. **Projection.** Weighted to the DNS-flagged Shopify storefronts in the Tranco top-200k (about 2,444), 15.6% (13.5–17.8%), about 382 stores (329–437, including the uncertainty of the frame size), show a banner yet send sandboxed ad requests before any choice; 20.9%, about 510 stores, keep sending them under GPC. These projections hold for the DNS frame only (apex or www host on Shopify), whose recall is about 83% (bucket-wise bounds 48–96%).
13. **What the pages saw of our crawler (added after referee review).** Every load showed automation: `navigator.webdriver` was true and the user agent contained 'HeadlessChrome' in 4,855 of 4,855 usable loads with a page probe, including all 99 Brave loads (Brave also ran headless). In the saved code of 24 consent-tool scripts, rules that match this fingerprint exist in Pandectes v5 (a bot rule that, when the merchant enables it, hides the banner and grants consent with reason 'bot'), in Usercentrics' loader (a user-agent test for HeadlessChrome and Playwright; its effect was not traced) and in Shopify's own banner (used for telemetry only in the saved build); OneTrust reads a server-side bot header (effect unknown), and Cookiebot and CookieHub test user agents against bot lists that do not match ours. The Pandectes signature (banner configured visible for California, banner element present but hidden, a 'marketing: yes' write without a click) appeared in both the Default and the GPC load of 5 stores (C020, S0140, S0173, S0265, S1079; all 118 Pandectes v5 loads had both automation signals), while 31 v5 stores configured as opt-in for California showed their banner and wrote nothing. Of the 22 Default loads in which a CMP switched a denying default to 'yes', 9 came with no detected banner: 3 of these are bot-rule stores and 6 are unexplained. Of 185 'yes' writes without a click in Default and GPC loads, 174 came before our first passive scroll and 11 after it (on 8 stores, 175–797 ms after one of our scrolls), where implied consent on scroll cannot be excluded. Detected consent tool vs banner shown in the Default load: Consentmo 104 stores, 1 banner; Usercentrics 21, 9; Didomi 14, 0 (element present but hidden on 11); full table `analysis/outputs/cmp_detection.csv` and `figures/tables/tableS_cmp_detection.tex`. Direction of the biases: a rule that grants consent to automated browsers makes pixels fire where a person would first have seen an opt-in banner (so such stores are excluded from the blocked-on-paper primary, and pre-choice rates on them are over-stated for human visitors); a rule that hides the banner from bots lowers the banner count; the recall re-check used the same browser (webdriver true and a HeadlessChrome user agent in 128 of 128 re-check loads), so it cannot find banners suppressed for bots.

## 2. Data and definitions

- **Sample.** 1,362 of 1,400 main-sample stores and 34 of 34 creator-stratum stores had a usable load in all three base arms (Default, Decline, GPC). 4,976 loads were attempted; 119 were not usable (38 stores), including 71 redirects to google.com and 29 missing product pages. 23 stores looked like bot blocking; nothing was done to get around it. Sampling funnel exclusions: 80 drawn sites were not live storefronts (47 headless or assets-only, 16 refused access (forbidden), 17 other errors); 38 storefronts were excluded (17 without a product page, 10 aimed at children, 7 duplicates, 4 adult content); 38 sampled stores were not captured in all three arms. Validation loads: repeats on 287 stores (274 usable main-sample stores) and Brave on 100 (99 usable).
- **Ad request.** A request to a vendor marked advertising in `crawl/vendor_endpoints.json`, whether a data beacon or a library (script) load; beacon-only and test-code-beacon versions of the headline rates are in Fig S6. CORS preflights (OPTIONS) are not ad requests: DevTools reports a worker fetch's preflight in the page session, where the crawler labelled it main-thread (318 Default requests, removed). **Sandboxed** means it was issued inside a Web Pixels Manager STRICT worker, a LAX iframe, or any frame nested inside a LAX iframe (the crawler labelled such frames by their own URL; following the saved frame tree moved 245 Default ad requests on 54 stores into the sandbox, and 52 whose frame URL was also used outside a sandbox were left out). No store-level sandboxed flag changed. **Relayed by Shopify** now means a main-thread request whose initiator stack holds only Shopify or store scripts; requests with a vendor library or another app script in the stack are ordinary main-thread tags.
- **Before any choice.** Every request of the Default load (no interaction), and requests of the Decline load before the click.
- **Decline click that worked.** The banner was no longer visible 2 s after the click, or Shopify recorded an explicit refusal. **Recorded refusal**: any Customer Privacy API reading after the click with `currentVisitorConsent.marketing` or `trackingConsent` equal to 'no'; other clicks are 'marketing still allowed' (state read, not refused) or 'unknown'. The old flag (`marketingAllowed()` false at the end of the load) was true on 202 of 242 clicked stores, but on 106 of them the region default already denied marketing before the click; on the 11 'denied' stores where the flag was not set, the state was unreadable, not 'yes'.
- **After Decline.** Primary: a sandbox created on the next page (the same URL reloaded after the click, observed 10 s after its load event) sent an ad request. Secondary: any sandboxed ad request after the click, same page or next page. Old sandboxes that send on the next page are unload beacons of the reload itself.
- **Equal windows.** For the Decline vs GPC comparison, Default and GPC count sandboxed requests within 10 s of the load event, the span the Decline arm's next page is observed for.
- **Region default.** Decoded from the navigation's `Server-Timing: _cmp` string at our California vantage point: 'allowed until choice' on 1,106 of 1,353 (81.7%, 95% CI 79.6–83.7%) of stores that returned one. Other US states and the EU get other defaults.
- **Integration group.** From the CMP that showed the banner (else the one detected), mapped with `crawl/cmp_signatures.json`: Shopify's own banner (mode N); a CMP that writes to Shopify's Customer Privacy API (modes A and C); blocker only, unknown or no CMP (modes B and X). It is a property of the consent product, not of the store.
- **Gate audit.** Configured pixels at risk (a declared purpose denied by the page's own consent string) are compared with the pixels that started. 'Pixel starts' are worker bootstraps, LAX iframes and OPEN main-thread scripts matched to Shopify's state at that moment.

### Figure 5 (do choices work?)
- 5a (same 233 working clicks, fresh page, 10 s windows): any vendor Default 45.5%, Decline 22.7%, GPC 17.6%; TikTok 19.7% / 6.4% / 1.3%; Microsoft 17.2% / 5.6% / 5.2%. Before the click on the Decline page: 43.3%.

- **Banner-detector recall (re-check crawl).** 129 main-sample stores were loaded once more (128 usable) with no interaction, and their screenshots were labelled blind to group, consent tool and detector result by the analysis assistant (human re-labelling pending). Applying the random rate to the pool gives about 40 missed banners in the main crawl (16–156), a recall of about 91% (72–96%), assuming a banner seen on the re-check was also shown in the original load. Counting the 12 banners found as banner stores gives 208 of 422 (49.3%, 95% CI 44.5–54.0%) against 595 of 940 (63.3%, 95% CI 60.2–66.3%).
- **Blocker activity on the blocklist stores (same re-check crawl, one load per store).** A blocker neutralized at least one script or iframe element on 18 of 21 (85.7%, 95% CI 65.4–95.0%) stores (Pandectes 15/15, Cookiebot 1/1, OneTrust 2/5). ## 6. Limitations and deviations

- **Concurrency above the agreed limit.** The first 578 loads ran at 6 concurrent browsers; a process from a 'coordinating session' then restarted the crawler at 10, and 4,398 loads ran that way. Its note says this was at the user's request; that could not be verified and it exceeds the task's politeness limit. Per-site pacing was unchanged. Run segment is a sensitivity row in Fig S6.
- **One vantage point, one day.** California residential IP, headless Chromium with its default user agent (Meta's library drops events for headless browsers, so Meta beacons are undercounted), Brave (also headless) only for the native-GPC subsample. Other US states and the EU get different Shopify region defaults, so the region-default shares hold for our vantage point only.
- **The crawler was visible as automation** (see 'What the pages saw of our crawler'): `navigator.webdriver` was true and the user agent said 'HeadlessChrome' in every usable load. Consent tools that react to this bias the results in known directions: Pandectes' bot rule (seen on 5 stores) hides the banner and grants consent, so pixels fire where a person would first meet an opt-in banner (such stores are excluded from the blocked-on-paper primary; their pre-choice firing overstates what a person sees); rules that hide a banner from bots (Usercentrics' user-agent test; OneTrust's server-side bot header, effect unknown) lower the banner count and so the detector's apparent recall; the recall re-check used the same browser and cannot find banners suppressed for bots. Our passive scrolls may also have triggered implied consent on scroll on the 8 stores whose 'yes' came after a scroll. A headful re-run without the automation flag on the affected stores would bound these effects; it needs a new crawl, a decision for the authors [AUTHORS].
- **Banner timing** comes from polling every 250 ms, not from an observer; the conservative estimate is primary and future crawls should timestamp visibility with a MutationObserver or IntersectionObserver.
- **Decline only on the first layer.** Banners without a first-layer reject were not clicked; one missed reject button in the audit suggests the finder slightly under-counts non-English reject labels. Banners inside iframes were not clicked. Failed forced clicks are excluded from the Decline analysis and reported separately.
- **Screenshot audits are not yet human.** Fig S4 and the forced-click audit were labelled by the analysis assistant from the saved screenshots; an independent human re-labelling of the same seeded sample is still required before the method can be called validated. Banner-detector recall was estimated from a later re-check load (see 'Checks added after the crawl'), at about 91% (72–96%), again with assistant labels and assuming a banner seen on the re-check was also shown in the original load.
- **Declared blocklists** are readable only for Pandectes, OneTrust and Cookiebot, so the vendor-matched 'blocked on paper' rate rests on 21 stores. Custom pixels are matched to vendors by name only (their code is not in the configuration).
- **Consent events of the Decline arm** were recorded on the last page only, so first-page state changes in Decline loads are known only from the once-a-second readings; the gate audit therefore excludes the first page of Decline loads (counts above).
- **Refusal signals** are read from request URLs only, as truncated in the request table (so flagged shares are lower bounds); bodies (e.g. Meta's data-processing options, TikTok's limited data use) are not in the request table.
- **Timing** mixes DevTools network timestamps and page clocks, the consent write time is the event dispatch, and data were collected under two concurrency levels.
- **Instrumentation pause.** The crawler attaches to every worker with waitForDebuggerOnStart, so each worker waits until its network capture is on: over 30,127 workers in 4,624 usable loads the wait was a median 5 ms (90th percentile 64 ms, 99th 816 ms, maximum 18,780 ms); for STRICT sandbox workers a median 6 ms, 90th percentile 69 ms, maximum 2,333 ms, and 695 of 20,672 (3.4%, 95% CI 3.1–3.6%) waited longer than one banner-polling interval. The pause delays sandboxed requests, so 'pixel before banner' is conservative; the race analysis times container creation, which the pause does not affect; store-level outcomes change only if a pause pushed a store's only request out of the observation window.
- **Integration mode is a product-level label** (21 consent products); the reference group is one product with an almost always denying default, so no model can separate mode from product or default.
- **Edge caches.** 7 main-sample stores (and Store C) served cached pages; their consent strings name other regions. They explain several gate-audit anomalies but not the headline rates (Fig S6).
- **Frame and projection.** The DNS frame (apex or www host on Shopify) has an estimated recall of about 83% (bucket-wise bounds 48–96%), and only 23% in the top bucket; it also excludes storefronts on shop. or store. subdomains (an estimated 1,828 in the top-200k). The projection covers the DNS-flagged Online Store storefronts only and is conditional on this frame.
