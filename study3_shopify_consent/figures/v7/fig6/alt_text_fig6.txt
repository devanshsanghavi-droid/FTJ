Four-panel figure: a line drawing with a dot plot, then three dot plots. (a) One creator-linked store, Store C. Left, a line drawing: a creator's video link, whose URL carries a per-video code, opens the store's landing page inside the browser; the page hands its URL to TikTok's and Google Ads' pixels, which Shopify runs in sandboxes isolated from the page, and red arrows carry the code from the sandboxes to the ad vendors outside the browser, each vendor named at the head of its arrow, while a grey arrow marks the page's other routes to them. Right, a dot plot with one row per page load, counting the ad vendors that received the code from a Shopify sandbox (red circles) and by any route (grey squares): before any choice the code reached 5 ad vendors, 2 of them (TikTok and Google Ads) from a sandbox; after a refusal, by a Decline click or under Global Privacy Control, none received it from a sandbox, but 4 and 3 vendors still received it by other routes. (b) Dot plot with 95% confidence intervals for the 34 creator-linked stores: the code reached an ad vendor on 79% of them and was sent from a sandbox on 56%. (c) Grey dots by vendor group with 95% confidence intervals: 63% of sandboxed ad beacons carried the full landing URL (a summary row at the bottom, whose confidence interval runs up through the vendor rows as a pale red band), from 42% (Reddit/other) to 96% (TikTok). (d) Grey dots on an agreement axis that does not start at zero, marked by a break at its left end: repeat loads, Brave's native Global Privacy Control and a screenshot audit agree with the crawler's measurements on 98% or more of stores or loads in every check.
