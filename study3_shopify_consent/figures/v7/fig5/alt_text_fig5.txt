Three-panel figure. (a) Dot plot with 95% confidence intervals: of the 231 stores where the Decline click worked, the share on which a newly created sandbox sent an advertising request is 46% under Default, 23% on the fresh page after Decline and 18% under the Global Privacy Control signal; in each of the seven ad vendor groups along the x axis Default is highest. Arms are told apart by marker shape and grey level. A refusal lowers the share but does not stop it. (b) Two thin horizontal stacked share bars, the classes named at the Decline bar, each bar's total and its 95% confidence interval drawn just under it. Shopify's gate held the new pixels that declare the refused purpose: none started after Decline (0 of 196 stores) or under GPC (0 of 7,458 'sale of data' entries), and a region default that denies 'marketing' held 2,206 of 2,234 such entries. Yet 33% of Decline stores and 21% of GPC stores still sent a sandboxed ad request, through pixels that started before the refusal (32 Decline stores, red), declare only purposes left allowed (grey), or declare none (dark grey). (c) Raster on a symmetric logarithmic time axis, one row per page load, of when each third-party 'marketing' sandbox started relative to a consent-management platform writing consent (amber line): when it wrote 'no', 525 of 535 sandboxes had already started, up to seconds earlier (red, left of the line, on a pale red area that runs from each load's first sandbox to the line); when it wrote 'yes', all 342 started after it, most within a few tens of milliseconds (blue-grey, right of the line). A key names the three tick colours, and a gap with cut marks on the row axis separates the 'no' loads from the 'yes' loads.
